## 🤖 The second broken thing: AI agents agree with each other

This is the subtle one, and it quietly kills most multi-agent products.

🔗 Ask one model to analyse a coin, then hand its answer to the next. The second one **anchors** on the first. Do that five times and you don't have five opinions — you have 1️⃣ opinion, repeated five times, wearing five hats. 🎩🎩🎩🎩🎩

🪞 It looks like a research team. It's an echo.

> 💡 **The insight the whole building is built on:** a research firm's value isn't the number of analysts. It's whether those analysts can genuinely **disagree.** ⚔️

## 🔒 So our analysts are forbidden from seeing each other's work

Five specialists study every coin, and each one runs **blind**.

🙈 The contract analyst doesn't know what the liquidity analyst found. The chart reader has never seen the narrative report. No shared context. No coordination. No "building on" a colleague's answer.

⚔️ They are **expected** to contradict each other — and when they do, that conflict IS the signal, not a problem to smooth over. One seat exists purely to referee them. 🧑‍⚖️

## 🧮 The part nobody else prints

We set those risk defaults by intuition first — sell half at target, trail tight. Sounded prudent.

Then we simulated it properly and found we were **wrong.** Memecoin returns are fat-tailed: the rare runners are the entire profit, and selling half at target quietly destroys the thing you're being paid for. **Every** scale-out setting reduced returns. So the shipped default changed: don't cut winners, trail wide.

With realistic trading costs, the desk has to hit about **18%** for the bot to break even:

📉 10% hit rate — loses money
📉 15% — loses money
⚖️ ~18% — break-even
📈 28% — profitable
🚀 40% — strongly profitable

⚠️ **And our desk has not published a call yet.** So its real hit rate is unknown — nobody, including us, can tell you which row you'd land on.
