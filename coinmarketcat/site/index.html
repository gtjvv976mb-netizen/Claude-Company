Standard procedure

How an agent works.

Every cat follows the same four steps. The details are CoinMarketCat's, because it is the only agent in the field.

  1. You set the limits

    Before it does anything, you tell it what it may spend per trade, what it may spend per day, when to take profit and what it may hunt. It checks those limits before every trade, not after.

  2. It watches

    A new agent starts in observe mode. It watches the market and writes down what it would have done — the misses as well as the hits — without spending anything, so you can judge it before you trust it.

  3. It pounces only inside them

    It trades only after you arm it, by typing a sentence that names your wallet and your numbers. Even then it pounces only when its rule says so, and a trade that would break one of your limits is refused.

  4. You can stop it any time

    Pause new entries, disarm it, or pull the hard stop — sell everything, buy nothing. Whatever it holds sits in a wallet you control, not with us.

The agency's principles

Four rules every cat is trained under.

They are why the agents look the way they do: slow to arm, quick to stop, and plain about what nobody has measured yet.

Your wallet, your limits

An agent trades from a wallet you control, inside numbers you typed. There is no account with us, no pooled fund and nothing to deposit with the agency. There is no agency server at all.

Observe first

Every agent ships in observe mode and keeps a book of what it would have done. Going live is a deliberate act: a sentence you type, naming your wallet and your numbers.

Honest numbers

No quoted returns, no win rates nobody measured, no user counts. What is unmeasured is called unmeasured — and the losing record CoinMarketCat learned from is printed beside its arming switch.

No key ever leaves your browser

Your Phantom key never reaches an agent. Autopilot's separate wallet is created inside your browser, encrypted under your passphrase, and never sent anywhere — there is nowhere to send it.
