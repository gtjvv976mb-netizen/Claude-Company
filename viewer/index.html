48The floor

One trade, sixteen specialists. Most coins die before anyone expensive sees them.

Every sweep starts wide — a few hundred coins, some minutes old — and gets narrower at every gate. The cheapest gate does most of the killing, and it is code, not a model. What survives is worked up by five analysts who cannot read each other, then attacked by a seat whose only job is to refute. Watch a sweep run.

46WALL-ST-E

The bot is yours. The desk never holds a key.

The building publishes tickets. A bot you install on your own machine reads them and trades from your own wallet, inside caps you set. The two halves are separated by construction, not by promise: there is no server-side signing, no custody, no browser RPC relay anywhere in the code — only an unsigned ticket, and a program on your side that proves the exact bytes before it signs anything.

The building · a server on Render

  1. Publishes the call

    Symbol, entry reference, stop, targets, the band's hold window, and the evidence it was made on. Unsigned. Nothing else leaves.

  2. Sizes it to your floor

    Your appetite, your categories, your launchpads, your bankroll — set in the tower, applied to every delivery, never seen by the house as money.

  3. Watches the exit

    Every open call is re-marked on its band's clock, key or no key, and the exit is published the same way the entry was.

Your machine · WALL-ST-E, one command to install

  1. Quotes, then proves the bytes

    The route is quoted, built, and then simulated exactly against the chain — the floor the calldata carries is read back out of the bytes and checked against what the chain would return. Bytes it cannot read, it will not sign.

  2. Journals before it discloses

    Every attempt is written to a local ledger before a signature exists, and marked "submitted" before the network can know. If the process dies mid-trade, the ledger says exactly what may have happened.

  3. Signs with a key that never leaves

    A key file on your disk, mode 600, read in-process. It is not in a repo, not in a response, not in a chat. The site cannot ask for it and would refuse it.

  4. Sends, and reconciles on two providers

    A receipt must be seen on both RPC providers before a fill is believed. No receipt after the deadline is unknown, not failed — reconciled by nonce, never blindly re-sent.

  5. Exits on the band's clock

    Nano and micro coins are flipped inside a session; medium and high are held longer. The stop, the target and the clock are the ticket's, enforced locally.

Custody never enters the building. Not the house's keys, not a tenant's. Every ticket, on every floor, leaves unsigned — and every trade is your own wallet's, entirely at your own discretion.

Solana What the bot does there

  • Routes through Jupiter; a stale transaction dies on its own after ~150 blocks, so a failed send can be rebuilt safely.
  • Pays a priority fee to get ahead; the network-fee ceiling is a hard refusal, not a hope.
  • Refuses Token-2022 mints with hazardous extensions before the quote is even asked.

Robinhood Chain What changes on an EVM chain

  • Routes through the KyberSwap aggregator; the slippage floor is read out of the calldata and checked against the chain's own answer, never the aggregator's.
  • A transaction never expires — it sits at a nonce. The bot owns its nonce sequence, cancels a dropped send with a 0-value self-transfer at the same nonce, and only then rebuilds. A nonce advanced for bytes never sent is handed back.
  • No priority fee exists to buy ordering; a block every ~100 ms, first come first served. A voided transaction (status 0x0, no logs) is never retried.
  • Every Robinhood Stock Token is refused as a position — by its proxy's beacon and by its name — and allowed only as the other side of a pool, in and out inside one trade.

44The lease

Fifty floors. One desk each.

The house works the crown. Everything beneath it leases. A leased floor copies the house desk's calls through its own mandate — appetite, categories, launchpads, bankroll — and the tenant's own bot trades them. The lease is paid in the access token; the trading, if you trade at all, happens in the chain's native coin from your own wallet. The two never mix, and the access token is never a position.

43Straight answers

The questions people actually ask.

Is this financial advice, or a promise of profit?

No. The desk is a research tool that publishes unsigned tickets and keeps a public record of how they did. The record on the Solana tower is shown above, live, and it currently says no edge is claimable. Nothing here states or implies performance.

Does the site ever hold my money or my keys?

No, by construction. There is no hosted signing, custody, or browser RPC relay in the code. A tenant trades from their own wallet; the optional bot runs on the tenant's own machine with a key file the site never sees. Connecting a wallet on this page signs a plain-text nonce to prove ownership — it is not a transaction and moves no funds.

What does the token do?

It opens a floor and pays rent. One million $CLAUDECO leases a floor of the tower; rent settles in the same token. The desk's research concerns other tokens — it does not cover, rate, price or trade this one, and the bot refuses it as a position by address. It is an access token, never the thing you trade.

How is a call actually made?

A sweep reads the launchpad's own feed. A free code screen kills most of it. Five analysts — forensics, liquidity, flow, narrative, technical — work the survivors blind to one another. A red-team seat attacks the proposal; the PM must answer the attack in writing; a compliance program can veto; only the CEO seat turns research into a call. What leaves is an unsigned ticket with a route, size, stop, targets and its band's hold window. Floor 48 above animates it.

What is the bot, and what will it never do?

WALL-ST-E is a self-hosted program that reads the building's tickets and trades them from your wallet inside caps you set. It quotes, builds and then simulates the exact bytes before signing; journals every attempt before the network can see it; reconciles receipts on two providers; and exits on the ticket's clock. It will never sign bytes whose floor it cannot read, never exceed a cap by rounding, never retry a voided transaction, and never take a position in a tokenised stock or the access token.

Which wallets work?

On Solana: Phantom and any Wallet-Standard wallet. On Robinhood Chain: any EIP-1193 wallet — Phantom's EVM provider, Rabby and Trust Wallet know chain 4663 already; MetaMask adds it on first connect (chain id 0x1237, RPC rpc.mainnet.chain.robinhood.com, explorer robinhoodchain.blockscout.com). The Robinhood Wallet app reaches the site through its in-app browser.

What do Grok and Codex do here?

Grok reads X — the one evidence source the desk could never reach on its own — once per shortlisted coin, and can be hired by a floor as that floor's managing director. Codex works outside the trading room as a read-only improvement engineer: it audits scorecards and prompt provenance and returns a proposal for human review. Neither can sign, size, order, or touch a wallet.

What is different about trading on Robinhood Chain?

Blocks land every ~100 ms in arrival order with no priority-fee auction, so speed cannot be bought; the sequencer can drop a transaction with no receipt, so the bot reconciles by nonce; the cost is a flat gas toll rather than a percentage of size, which punishes tiny clips; launches are PONS bonding curves with a 99%→0% tax over the first five seconds that makes block-0 sniping pointless by design. The edge on this chain is selection among the ~1.5% that graduate, not speed — which is exactly what a research desk is for.

Where is the code?

Both towers are public on GitHub: the Solana original and the Robinhood fork, linked in the lobby below. The executor, the seats' charters, the evidence contract, the deployment runbook and the ledger of lessons are all in the open.
